<h2><strong>The valley people come for</strong></h2><p>Hunza runs along the Karakoram Highway in northern Gilgit-Baltistan, from around 2,000m at the river to nearly 5,000m at the Khunjerab Pass on the Chinese border. Rakaposhi, 7,788m, and Ultar Sar, 7,388m, rise directly above the valley, and the terraced fields, irrigation channels and stone villages built into those slopes are as remarkable as the peaks themselves.</p><p>It is the most visited part of northern Pakistan, and still one of the most rewarding, because almost everything worth seeing is within an easy drive of Karimabad.</p><h2><strong>Getting there</strong></h2><p>Hunza is reached by road along the Karakoram Highway: roughly two to three hours north of Gilgit, or a long two-day drive from Islamabad broken at Chilas or Naran. Flights to Gilgit exist but are weather-dependent, so most itineraries plan for the road and adjust if a seat becomes available.</p><h2><strong>What to see</strong></h2><p><strong>Baltit Fort</strong> stands above Karimabad, around seven hundred years old, restored in the 1990s and open to visitors — the view from its upper windows down the valley is the photograph everyone takes home. <strong>Altit Fort</strong>, lower down and older still, sits above the river with a restored garden and a village of narrow lanes around it.</p><p><strong>Attabad Lake</strong> fills the valley floor beyond Karimabad, formed by a landslide in 2010 that dammed the Hunza river. The water is an improbable blue, and boats run from the shore.</p><p><strong>The Passu Cones</strong>, the row of serrated peaks above Passu village, mark the upper valley, with the Passu and Batura glaciers close to the road and the much-photographed Hussaini suspension bridge nearby.</p><p><strong>Eagle's Nest</strong> above Duikar gives the sunrise and sunset view over the whole valley. <strong>Khunjerab Pass</strong>, at 4,693m, is the highest paved border crossing in the world and a half-day drive from Karimabad, open in the warmer months.</p><h2><strong>How long to stay</strong></h2><p>Two nights is the minimum and leaves you wishing for more. <strong>Four nights in Karimabad</strong> covers the forts, Attabad, the upper valley and a sunrise at Duikar without rushing. Our <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" href="/tours/hunza-valley-heritage-tour/">Hunza valley tour</a> takes ten days from Islamabad and back, which allows the Karakoram Highway to be enjoyed rather than endured.</p><h2><strong>When to go</strong></h2><p><strong>Late March to mid-April</strong> is blossom season, when apricot and cherry turn the valley white and pink below the snow — our <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" href="/tours/spring-blossom-tour/">spring blossom tour</a> follows it. <strong>May to September</strong> gives warm, clear days and opens Khunjerab. <strong>Mid-October to early November</strong> brings the autumn colour that photographers return for. Winter is cold, quiet and beautiful, with the valley still accessible.</p><h2><strong>Where to stay</strong></h2><p>Karimabad has the widest choice, from guest houses to comfortable hotels with valley views, and a handful of heritage properties. Gulmit and Passu in the upper valley are quieter and well placed for the glaciers. In blossom and autumn weeks the best rooms are booked months ahead.</p><h2><strong>Practical notes</strong></h2><p>Hunza is relaxed, literacy is high and the valley is used to visitors, which makes it one of the easiest places in Pakistan to travel. Mobile coverage is reasonable along the highway. Altitude is modest in Karimabad but Khunjerab at nearly 4,700m is high enough that some visitors feel it on the drive up. Carry cash; card facilities are limited.</p><h2><strong>Journeys in and around Hunza</strong></h2><p>The <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" href="/trekking/rakaposhi-base-camp-trek/">Rakaposhi base camp trek</a> starts from Minapin on the way into the valley, and the <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" href="/trekking/rush-lake-trek/">Rush Lake trek</a> climbs from Nagar on the other side of the river.

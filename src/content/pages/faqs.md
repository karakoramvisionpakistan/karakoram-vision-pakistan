<p>Straight answers to the questions travellers ask us most. If yours is not here, <a href="/contact-us/">write to us</a> and our Skardu team will reply.</p>
<h2>Planning and booking</h2>
<h3>Can you plan a trip around my own interests and dates?</h3>
<p>Yes. No single package can cover everything Pakistan offers, so most of our trips run on private dates and are shaped around the people travelling. Tell us what you want to see or climb, how many days you have and your experience, and we will send a written itinerary to fit.</p>
<h3>How do I book?</h3>
<ol>
<li>Contact us with your route, dates and group size. We can hold a place for seven days while you decide.</li>
<li>We send a written proposal with the itinerary, price, payment schedule and booking terms.</li>
<li>Confirm by returning the booking details and the deposit. Names must match your passports exactly, including first names you do not normally use.</li>
<li>Send us your travel insurance details. We then confirm everything by email and, before departure, send final joining instructions.</li>
</ol>
<h3>Why are prices shown as "on request"?</h3>
<p>Permits, flights, hotels and transport costs change from season to season, and the price depends on group size and route. A current written quote is more reliable than a fixed figure that may be out of date.</p>
<h3>Is there a minimum group size?</h3>
<p>Most of our trips run privately for two people or more. If you are travelling alone, ask us: we can quote a private trip or check whether you can join another group.</p>
<h3>What is the cancellation policy?</h3>
<p>Cancellation charges depend on how close to departure you cancel and on what has already been paid to hotels, airlines and permit offices. The full terms are in our <a href="/terms-and-conditions/">terms and conditions</a> and <a href="/refund-policy/">refund policy</a>.</p>
<h2>When to go</h2>
<h3>When is the best time to visit northern Pakistan?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Treks and expeditions:</strong> mid-June to the end of August, when the high passes and glaciers are open.</li>
<li><strong>Blossom:</strong> late March to mid-April in Hunza and Baltistan, see the <a href="/tours/spring-blossom-tour/">Spring Blossom Tour</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Autumn colours:</strong> October, when the poplars and orchards turn gold.</li>
<li><strong>Winter:</strong> valley and cultural trips are possible, but snow closes the high passes.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Do you guarantee flights and road access?</h3>
<p>No operator can. The Skardu flight only operates in clear weather and mountain roads can close after rain or snow. Our itineraries keep a spare day for this, and we discuss the alternatives with you before booking.</p>
<h2>Trekking with us</h2>
<h3>I have never trekked before. Can I trek in the Karakoram?</h3>
<p>If you are healthy and exercise regularly, yes. Start with a shorter, lower trek such as the <a href="/trekking/nangma-valley-trek/">Nangma Valley trek</a>. Our itineraries gain height slowly with rest days built in, which is what lets most people adapt well to altitude.</p>
<h3>What is a camping trek?</h3>
<p>On most Karakoram routes there are no lodges, so we camp. You sleep two to a tent, and a guide, cook and porters travel with the group, carry the camp and prepare all meals.</p>
<h3>Why travel with a local guide?</h3>
<p>A guide from these valleys knows the route, the weather and the people. They speak the local languages, open doors you would otherwise walk past, carry first aid, and know how to keep your impact on the villages and the mountains small.</p>
<h3>What equipment should I bring?</h3>
<p>It depends on the season and the trip. Start with our <a href="/equipment-checklist/">equipment checklist</a>; we confirm the exact list for your trip before departure, and much of it can be rented in Skardu.</p>
<h2>Visas, permits, insurance and life on the trek</h2>
<h3>Do I need a visa for Pakistan?</h3>
<p>Yes. Pakistan issues separate visa categories: a Tourist Visa for tourists, a Trekking Visa for trekkers and a Mountaineering Visa for climbers, and you should apply under the category that matches your trip. Most nationalities apply online through the official e-visa portal. An application normally needs an invitation letter and tour confirmation from a licensed Pakistani operator, which we issue once your booking is confirmed. Apply four to six weeks ahead.</p>
<h3>Is travel insurance compulsory?</h3>
<p>Yes. You must arrive with your own policy, and it has to cover search and rescue at the altitude of your trip as well as medical treatment and repatriation. Ordinary holiday insurance excludes both. Global Rescue is the policy we see most often, and several other insurers cover high-altitude search and rescue in the Karakoram. Send us the policy number and the insurer's emergency line before you travel: in an emergency we contact your insurer directly, and that call cannot wait while paperwork is found.</p>
<h3>Are the porters and staff insured?</h3>
<p>Yes. Karakoram Vision Pakistan insures every member of staff and every porter through Alpha Insurance before a trek or expedition begins. Porters in Gilgit-Baltistan do not normally carry insurance of their own, so this is arranged and paid for by us rather than left to them. Porters are paid at the official Gilgit-Baltistan rates, and above them where the ground or the season justifies it.</p>
<h3>How much do porters carry, and what do I carry?</h3>
<p>A porter carries 25 kg on a normal trek and 20 kg where the route crosses a high pass, which is harder ground and thinner air. Your own duffle bag can weigh up to 25 kg and is carried for you. You walk with a daypack holding water, layers, camera and whatever you need during the day.</p>
<h3>Who are the guides, and what are they qualified in?</h3>
<p>Our guides hold government licences, most are fluent in English and several speak other languages, and the majority are certified by the Alpine Club of Pakistan. They are from these valleys rather than brought in for the season, which matters when the weather turns or a route has changed since last year.</p>
<h3>How fit do I need to be?</h3>
<p>Fit enough to walk six to eight hours on rough ground, several days in a row, carrying a daypack. Most people who prepare succeed; most people who do not, struggle by the fourth day. Three to four months of regular hill walking with a loaded pack is the preparation that works. Technical climbing skill is only needed on the trips where we say so.</p>
<h3>What happens about altitude?</h3>
<p>Our itineraries gain height gradually and include rest days where the profile needs them. Mild headache and poor sleep are common above 3,500m; genuine altitude sickness is not, and it is treated by descending. Guides carry a first aid kit and oxygen, watch the group daily, and will send someone down when that is the safe decision. Expeditions carry considerably more medical equipment. Never hide symptoms to keep up with the group.</p>
<h3>Is there any way to call for help on the trek?</h3>
<p>Yes. A satellite phone and radios travel on every trek, not only on expeditions. Mobile signal reaches Skardu and the larger valleys but stops well before the glaciers, so the satellite phone is the link for weather, logistics and emergencies, and the radios keep the group in contact when it is spread along a long day.</p>
<h3>What permits are required for trekking and climbing in Gilgit-Baltistan?</h3>
<p>For trekking and mountaineering in restricted areas of Gilgit-Baltistan, visitors are required to obtain the relevant permits and pay the applicable fees. A trekking permit is required for trekking up to 6,500 metres in restricted areas; it is issued by the Gilgit-Baltistan Tourism Department, and the applicable trekking fees must be paid. Visitors entering the Central Karakoram National Park are required to pay the applicable CKNP fee, which is deposited with the Central Karakoram National Park authorities. For climbing peaks above 6,500 metres a climbing permit is required, issued through the relevant government authorities, and the applicable peak royalty fees must also be paid. For our expeditions the royalty is included in the price. We handle the applications, the registration in Skardu and the roadhead arrangements.</p>
<h3>How do I book and pay?</h3>
<p>Send your dates, group size and the trip you have in mind, and you receive a written itinerary and a full cost breakdown before any payment is discussed. A deposit of 50 per cent confirms the booking and starts the permit and visa paperwork. The remaining 50 per cent is paid after you reach Islamabad and before you travel to Skardu, in euros or US dollars, or by bank transfer. Prices differ from trip to trip, so there is no single rate to quote.</p>
<h3>What equipment can I hire instead of bringing?</h3>
<p>We hire out sleeping bags, and a wide range of trekking and climbing gear is available to rent in Skardu, so you do not have to fly with everything. Tell us what you would rather hire when you book and it is set aside before you arrive. Boots are the exception: bring your own, worn in.</p>
<h3>How much should I tip?</h3>
<p>Tips are not fixed and are entirely at your discretion. What usually happens is that the group agrees an amount with the guide at the end of the trip and it is shared among the crew. Nobody will ask you for a figure beforehand.</p>
<h3>What is the food like, and can you cater for my diet?</h3>
<p>A kitchen crew travels with the group — one or more cooks with assistants and waiters, depending on the size of the party — and meals are prepared in a kitchen tent: breakfast, a packed or hot lunch, and a cooked dinner, built around local staples with plenty of rice, lentils, bread, eggs and seasonal vegetables. Tell us about vegetarian, halal, allergy or other requirements when you book and they are planned for rather than improvised on the glacier.</p>
<h3>Where do we sleep?</h3>
<p>Hotels in Skardu and at the roadhead, and camping on trek, with a mess tent and toilet tents kept entirely separate from the sleeping tents. A single tent can be arranged if you would rather not share, and single occupancy in hotels is possible for a supplement.</p>
<h3>How do I get to Skardu, and what if the flight is cancelled?</h3>
<p>There are flights from Islamabad to Skardu, and they depend on the weather in the mountains, so cancellation is normal rather than exceptional. The alternative is the road, about two days along the Karakoram Highway and the Skardu road, and a journey worth having in its own right. Build spare days into your travel at both ends and hold a flexible ticket home.</p>
<h3>Is it safe to trek in Gilgit-Baltistan?</h3>
<p>Gilgit-Baltistan is the part of Pakistan built around mountain tourism, and these valleys have hosted foreign teams for decades. Foreign trekkers are registered before leaving Skardu, our staff travel with the group throughout, and a satellite phone and radios are carried on every trip. Check your own government's travel advice as well, since advisories change and your insurance may depend on them.</p>
<h3>Can you run this on private dates?</h3>
<p>Yes. Almost every trip can run for a couple, a family or a private group on dates that suit you, and the cost changes with the number of people. Send the dates you are considering and you will get a written itinerary and price for that group size.</p>

<p>This is the list we send to our own guests before a trek or an expedition in the Karakoram. It comes from what actually gets used on the Baltoro, in the Hushe valleys and at the 8,000 m base camps, and from what people tell us they wish they had brought. Treat it as a starting point and adjust it to your trip, the season and your own experience. If you think something is missing, tell us and we will add it.</p>
<h2><strong>How to use this list</strong></h2>
<ul><li><strong>Treks</strong> (K2 base camp, Gondogoro La, Biafo Hispar, Nangma, Thalle La and similar): take everything except the <em>Climbing and expedition equipment</em> section.</li><li><strong>Expeditions and trekking peaks</strong>: take the full list. We confirm the technical kit for your peak in the pre-departure briefing.</li><li><strong>Weight</strong>: a porter carries 25 kg on a normal trek and 20 kg where the route crosses a high pass. Pack your main duffel to the weight we agree before departure and keep what you need during the day in your rucksack.</li><li><strong>Buying or renting in Skardu</strong>: sleeping bags, down jackets, crampons and gas can be bought or rented in Skardu's mountaineering shops, but quality and sizes vary. Bring your boots and anything worn next to the skin from home.</li><li><strong>Medicine</strong>: speak to your doctor before you travel about altitude, any regular medication and which prescription medicines to carry. The first-aid list below is a reminder, not medical advice.</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Travel documents</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Passport valid for at least six months, with your Pakistan visa</li><li>Four passport-size photos</li><li>Travel insurance policy, including emergency evacuation, and the insurer's emergency number</li><li>Credit or debit cards, and some US dollars in cash</li><li>Contact details for your family or company</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Bags</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Rucksack, 45-55 litres (30-40 litres is enough on treks)</li><li>Duffel bag, 90-120 litres (two for expeditions)</li><li>Waterproof stuff sacks, two large and two small</li><li>Rubbish bags to carry your own waste out</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Upper body</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Lightweight thermal tops (2-3)</li><li>Midweight tops (2-3)</li><li>Heavyweight top (1-2)</li><li>Fleece jacket</li><li>Windproof jacket</li><li>Waterproof rain jacket</li><li>Down jacket</li><li>Cotton T-shirts for base camp (2-3)</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Lower body</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Lightweight long underwear (2-3 pairs)</li><li>Midweight long underwear (2-3 pairs)</li><li>Heavyweight long underwear (1 pair)</li><li>Trekking trousers, plus fleece or wool trousers for the evenings</li><li>Waterproof trousers</li><li>Down trousers (expeditions)</li><li>Underwear (5-7)</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Head and hands</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Sun hat or cap with a neck flap</li><li>Fleece or wool hat</li><li>Balaclava</li><li>Neck gaiters (2-3)</li><li>Thin fleece gloves (2 pairs)</li><li>Windproof fleece gloves</li><li>Heavy mittens with a waterproof outer</li><li>Summit gloves (expeditions)</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Footwear</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Broken-in trekking boots, with spare laces</li><li>Camp shoes or sandals</li><li>Trekking socks (4-6 pairs) and thin liner socks</li><li>Gaiters (optional)</li><li>Summit boots and summit socks (expeditions)</li><li>Rock shoes (big-wall and rock objectives)</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Sleeping</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Sleeping bag rated to -10°C or lower for treks and base camp</li><li>Down sleeping bag rated -20°C to -40°C for high camps (expeditions)</li><li>Sleeping mat (we supply a foam mat; an inflatable one adds comfort)</li><li>Small pillow</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Sun and on the trail</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Sunglasses and glacier glasses with full UV protection</li><li>Sunscreen SPF 50 and lip balm with SPF</li><li>Trekking poles</li><li>Head torch with spare or rechargeable batteries</li><li>Power bank: there is no mains electricity on trek</li><li>Water bottles (two) or a hydration bladder, plus purification tablets or a filter</li><li>Pocket knife</li><li>Light umbrella or poncho</li><li>Map or guidebook</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Washing and toiletries</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Hand sanitiser</li><li>Wet wipes and toilet paper</li><li>Toothbrush and toothpaste</li><li>Biodegradable soap and shampoo</li><li>Small quick-dry towel</li></ul>
<h2><strong>First-aid kit</strong></h2>
<ul><li>Plasters, blister dressings and a crepe bandage for sprains</li><li>Antiseptic cream and iodine</li><li>Paracetamol and ibuprofen</li><li>Throat lozenges</li><li>Oral rehydration salts</li><li>Anti-diarrhoea tablets</li><li>Sterile syringe and needle set</li><li>Prescription medicines your doctor recommends, for example an antibiotic for traveller's diarrhoea and acetazolamide (Diamox) for altitude</li></ul>
<h2><strong>Climbing and expedition equipment</strong></h2>
<p>Only for expeditions and trekking peaks. We confirm the exact list for your peak before departure.</p>
<ul><li>Climbing helmet</li><li>Harness</li><li>Ice axe</li><li>Crampons fitted to your boots</li><li>Ascender (jumar)</li><li>Belay device</li><li>Locking carabiners (3) and non-locking carabiners (3)</li><li>Tape slings (2) and a prusik loop</li><li>Down suit for 8,000 m peaks</li><li>Snow goggles</li><li>Hand warmers</li><li>Oxygen mask and regulator, where oxygen is part of your plan</li><li>Team kit we arrange with you: fixed rope, snow bars, ice screws, radios, satellite phone, stoves and gas for high camps</li></ul>

<h2><strong>Where Skardu is, and why everything starts here</strong></h2>
<p>Skardu sits at around 2,200 to 2,500m where the Indus and Shigar rivers meet, ringed by the Karakoram. It is the capital of Baltistan and the staging post for a remarkable share of the world's high mountains: K2, Broad Peak, the Gasherbrums, Masherbrum and the Baltoro glacier are all reached from here, along with the Deosai plateau, the Shigar and Khaplu valleys and the Hushe road.</p>
<p>For travellers who are not climbing, Skardu is simply one of the most spectacular places in Pakistan to spend a week — lakes, cold deserts, forts and villages, all within a short drive of the town.</p>

<h2><strong>Getting there</strong></h2>
<p>There are two ways in. The <strong>flight from Islamabad</strong> takes under an hour and passes Nanga Parbat on the way; it is one of the great short flights anywhere, and it is weather-dependent, so cancellations happen in every season. The <strong>road</strong> takes roughly eighteen to twenty-four hours along the Karakoram Highway and then the Indus valley, usually split over two days with a night at Chilas or Naran.</p>
<p>We plan every Skardu itinerary with a road contingency, and we recommend keeping a spare day at the end of a trip before any international connection.</p>

<h2><strong>What to see</strong></h2>
<p><strong>The lakes.</strong> Shangrila, also called Lower Kachura, with its red-roofed resort on the water, and Upper Kachura a short walk beyond it — quieter, deeper and greener. Both are within half an hour of town.</p>
<p><strong>The cold deserts.</strong> Katpana and the larger Sarfaranga are high-altitude sand dunes sitting between snow peaks, a landscape that surprises most first-time visitors. Sarfaranga is at its best in the late afternoon.</p>
<p><strong>Kharpocho Fort</strong> stands above the town on a rock spur, with a steep walk up and a view over the whole Skardu valley and the braided Indus.</p>
<p><strong>The Shigar valley</strong> begins forty-five minutes away, with its restored fort-palace and the long road toward Askole. <strong>Khaplu</strong> is two and a half to three hours east. <strong>Deosai</strong> climbs south from town when the road is open.</p>

<h2><strong>How long to stay</strong></h2>
<p>Three days covers the lakes, the deserts and the fort at an easy pace. <strong>Seven days</strong> is the comfortable length, adding Shigar, Khaplu and either Deosai or Manthokha falls in Kharmang — this is what our <a href="/tours/skardu-shigar-valley-explorer/">Skardu and Shigar valley tour</a> covers. Trekkers and expedition teams usually allow two or three nights in Skardu at each end for permits, briefing and equipment.</p>

<h2><strong>When to go</strong></h2>
<p>April brings blossom to the valley; June to September opens every road including Deosai; mid-October brings autumn colour and the clearest air of the year. Winter is cold and quiet, with limited hotel heating and an unreliable flight. Our guide to the <a href="/blog/best-time-to-visit-skardu/">best time to visit Skardu</a> goes through the seasons month by month.</p>

<h2><strong>Where to stay</strong></h2>
<p>Skardu has the widest range of accommodation in Baltistan, from simple guest houses to comfortable hotels and the lakeside resorts at Kachura. In peak season, July and August, rooms fill early with domestic travellers, so booking ahead matters. Shigar and Khaplu both have heritage accommodation in restored buildings for those who want a night in each valley.</p>

<h2><strong>Practical notes</strong></h2>
<p>Mobile coverage is good in town and patchy beyond it; there is none on the trekking routes. Cash is essential — ATMs exist in Skardu but not in the side valleys. Foreign travellers are registered for trekking routes before departure, which we handle. Altitude is modest in town itself, but Deosai at 4,000m is high enough that some people feel it, so the plateau is best visited after a day or two in Skardu.</p>

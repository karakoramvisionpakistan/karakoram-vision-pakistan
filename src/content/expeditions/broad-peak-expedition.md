<h2><strong>About this journey</strong></h2><p>Broad Peak, 8,051 metres, stands immediately beside K2 at the head of the Baltoro and shares its approach, its weather and very nearly its base camp. It is the twelfth highest mountain on earth and the one most teams in Pakistan attempt first, because the normal route carries no sustained technical difficulty. What it does carry is a summit ridge that has turned back more parties than almost any other feature in the Karakoram.</p><p>The programme runs fifty days door to door, of which thirty-two are spent at or above base camp. The walk in is the classic Baltoro approach; if you want to know that ground before you arrive, our <a href="/trekking/k2-base-camp-trek/">K2 base camp trek page</a> covers every stage of it.</p><h2><strong>The route</strong></h2><p>The normal line climbs the west face to the col on the main ridge, with camps at roughly 5,800, 6,400 and 7,100 metres. None of it is technically hard by modern standards: snow and ice slopes, some fixed rope on the steeper sections, and a long but straightforward trudge between camps. The face is exposed to afternoon sun and to wind off K2, and the camps are small and awkwardly placed.</p><p>From the col the route turns along the summit ridge, and this is where Broad Peak becomes a serious mountain. The ridge is narrow, corniced in places, and runs for well over a kilometre at around eight thousand metres. It is not steep. It is simply very long, very high, very cold, and almost always attempted at the end of an already long day.</p><h2><strong>The foresummit, and why it matters</strong></h2><p>This is the single most important thing to understand about Broad Peak before you book an expedition to it.</p><p>The ridge throws up a false top at about 8,030 metres, twenty metres below the true summit and perhaps an hour or two short of it along the ridge. From the foresummit the real summit is visible but looks close and inconsequential, and the ground between the two is corniced and tiring. A great many climbers over the years have turned around at the foresummit, in good faith, believing they had finished the mountain.</p><p>Several well-known claimed ascents have later been reassessed on exactly this point. If you are climbing Broad Peak for the record as well as for the experience, build the extra hours into your summit-day plan and discuss the turnaround time with your team in advance rather than on the ridge.</p><p>Note also that <strong>Broad Peak Central</strong>, 8,011 metres, is a separate summit on the same massif and is not the same mountain.</p><h2><strong>The first ascent</strong></h2><p>Broad Peak was climbed on 9 June 1957 by a four-man Austrian team: Marcus Schmuck, Fritz Wintersteller, Kurt Diemberger and Hermann Buhl. They did it without high-altitude porters and without supplementary oxygen, carrying their own loads, which in 1957 was an extraordinary way to climb an eight-thousander and is still regarded as one of the cleanest first ascents in the Karakoram.</p><p>Buhl, who had made the first ascent of Nanga Parbat four years earlier and alone, died three weeks later on Chogolisa, a few kilometres south across Concordia, when a cornice gave way in poor visibility. His body was never recovered. You pass beneath Chogolisa on the walk in, and most teams are told the story somewhere around Goro.</p><h2><strong>A first eight-thousander &mdash; with one caution</strong></h2><p>Broad Peak is a reasonable choice for a first peak above eight thousand metres, and we say so plainly: the route is within reach of a competent, fit, well-acclimatised alpinist with high-altitude experience on a 6,000 or 7,000-metre peak.</p><p>The caution is the ridge, and the caution is honest. A first-time eight-thousand-metre climber arriving at the col after seven hours, with the summit an hour and a half further along a corniced ridge and the afternoon weather building over K2, faces a decision that experience makes easier. Plan the summit day with that moment in mind. More expeditions on this mountain are decided by turnaround discipline than by fitness.</p><h2><strong>Combining with K2 or a Gasherbrum peak</strong></h2><p>Teams frequently put Broad Peak together with a second objective in the same season, and we can arrange it as one permit and logistics package rather than two expeditions.</p><p><strong>With K2</strong> is the common pairing, because the two base camps are a few hours apart on the Godwin-Austen glacier and a team acclimatised on Broad Peak is well placed for a K2 window later in the season. <strong>With a Gasherbrum peak</strong> works too, although the Gasherbrum base camp sits on a different glacier and the move costs days. Tell us at the planning stage, because the permit royalty, the liaison officer and the porter arrangements all change.</p><h2><strong>Season</strong></h2><p><strong>20 June to 15 August.</strong> Broad Peak windows cluster in July. Teams arriving at base camp in late June have time to rotate properly before the main settled spell; teams arriving in late July are gambling on a short tail of the season.</p><p>The Baltoro approach is at its most reliable in the same window, which is convenient, and the Skardu flight is at its least reliable throughout, which is not. The programme carries spare days at both ends for that reason.</p><h2><strong>Broad Peak in winter</strong></h2><p>Broad Peak held out against winter ascent for longer than most of the eight-thousanders, and the story of how it fell is one that climbers here still talk about at base camp.</p><p>A Polish expedition reached the summit on 5 March 2013: Adam Bielecki, Artur Ma&#322;ek, Maciej Berbeka and Tomasz Kowalski. Berbeka had unfinished business with the mountain &mdash; in 1988 he had reached the foresummit in winter and come down believing he had topped out, and it was only later established that he had stopped twenty metres short. Twenty-five years on, he went back and finished it.</p><p>All four reached the top late in the day. Only two came down. Berbeka and Kowalski died on the descent, and the inquiry that followed in Poland examined the summit timings, the decision to continue, and the gap that had opened between the pairs. It is a hard piece of reading and it is directly relevant to anyone planning a summit day on this mountain, for one reason: the thing that killed them was the same long ridge and the same late turnaround that the foresummit question creates in summer.</p><p>None of this is offered as discouragement. Broad Peak in July is a different proposition from Broad Peak in March. It is offered because the mountain&rsquo;s own history makes the single most important point about climbing it better than any safety briefing can.</p><h2><strong>What the numbers say</strong></h2><p>Broad Peak sees more traffic than any other eight-thousander in Pakistan and the summit rate in a good season is respectable &mdash; which still leaves a meaningful proportion of teams going home without a top, usually because the weather never produced a workable window rather than because anyone was unfit.</p><p>Deaths on the mountain cluster in two places: the descent from the ridge in deteriorating weather, and avalanche on the face. Neither is addressed by being stronger. Both are addressed by turnaround discipline and by crossing the face early. We make that point before you book rather than after.</p><h2><strong>Base camp, and the K2 neighbourhood</strong></h2><p>Broad Peak base camp sits on the Godwin-Austen glacier a short walk from K2 base camp, and in July the two together form the busiest stretch of ice in the Karakoram. That has practical consequences worth knowing before you arrive.</p><p>It means company: several expeditions, a rope-fixing effort on K2 that the whole glacier watches, and a flow of information about conditions, weather forecasts and who is where on which mountain. It also means a working camp rather than a quiet one, with generators, drying lines and a certain amount of traffic past the tents.</p><p>More usefully, it means a Broad Peak team sits beside the best-informed group of people in the range. Teams acclimatising here routinely swap forecast data and route information with K2 parties, and climbers who came for one mountain have been known to leave having attempted the other. We run base camp with a cook, kitchen and dining tent and communications throughout, and the Gilkey Memorial is a short walk up the glacier for anyone who wants to see it.</p><h2><strong>Planning notes</strong></h2><p>A full Broad Peak expedition framework with Pakistan permit support, Baltoro approach, base-camp services and high-altitude logistics.

<h2><strong>About this journey</strong></h2><p>The Trango Towers rise above the Baltoro glacier as some of the largest granite walls on earth. Great Trango carries one of the world's highest cliffs; the Nameless Tower beside it is among the most sought-after big-wall objectives anywhere. Teams have been coming here since the 1970s and new lines are still being climbed.</p><p><strong>What this programme is.</strong> Thirty-three days from Islamabad, covering the Baltoro trek in to base camp below the towers, the climbing period, and the return. Longer stays can be arranged for teams planning more than one route.</p><p><strong>Who it is for.</strong> Experienced big-wall and aid climbers who are self-sufficient on vertical ground at altitude. The approach is a normal Baltoro trek; everything above base camp is on you.</p><p><strong>What we provide.</strong> Climbing permit, park fees and liaison officer, the mountaineering visa invitation letter, transport and hotels, porters to base camp, and base camp with cook and kitchen for the duration of your stay on the wall.</p><h2><strong>The Trango group</strong></h2>
<p>The towers stand on a single ridge on the north side of the Baltoro glacier, between the Trango and Dunge glaciers, about a day's walk above Paiju. Two summits draw most teams. <strong>Great Trango Tower</strong> (6,286 m) is a broad massif whose east face is one of the tallest near-vertical walls on earth. <strong>Trango Tower</strong>, better known as <strong>Nameless Tower</strong> (6,239 m), is the slender spire beside it, the one every trekker on the way to K2 stops to photograph. Smaller summits along the same ridge, including Trango Monk, Trango Pulpit and Trango Castle, carry their own hard routes.</p>
<h2><strong>A short climbing history</strong></h2>
<p>Nameless Tower was first climbed in 1976 by a British team led by Joe Brown, and Great Trango a year later by an American team that included Galen Rowell and John Roskelley. Since then the group has become one of the main proving grounds for big-wall climbing at altitude. The Slovenian (Yugoslav) Route of 1987 is the most repeated line on Nameless Tower, and Eternal Flame, opened in 1989 by Kurt Albert and Wolfgang Güllich, became a landmark of free climbing at over 6,000 m when Alexander and Thomas Huber freed it in 2009. New lines are still being added on both towers.</p>
<h2><strong>Choosing your objective</strong></h2>
<p>Tell us the tower and the route when you first contact us. The position of base camp, the number of porter loads, the size of the kitchen team and the length of the climbing window are all planned around it. Most teams on Nameless Tower aim for the established crack lines on the south-east side; Great Trango's long west and south routes need a different camp and more time. We do not give technical route advice, but we can put you in touch with climbers who have been on the wall recently.</p>
<h2><strong>Permits and paperwork</strong></h2>
<p>The towers lie inside the Central Karakoram National Park, so the expedition needs a climbing permit, park entry and, where the rules require it, a liaison officer. We apply for all of these, send the invitation letter you need for a mountaineering visa, and handle registration in Skardu. Start the process at least three months before departure; permits take time and the summer slots fill early.</p>
<h2><strong>When to go</strong></h2>
<p>June to August. Most teams reach base camp in late June or early July to make the most of the long, settled weeks of midsummer; our scheduled departure runs from 22 June to 21 July 2027, and private dates are possible across the season.</p>

<h2><strong>Pack for altitude, not for a country</strong></h2>
<p>The mistake most first-time visitors make is packing for &quot;Pakistan&quot;. Islamabad in July is hot and humid; the same week at a camp on the Baltoro is below freezing at night. What you need depends almost entirely on how high you will sleep and for how long.</p>
<p>Work out your highest overnight altitude, then pack for that, and add light clothes for the first and last days in the city.</p>

<h2><strong>Documents and money</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li>Passport with at least six months validity, plus photocopies kept separately.</li>
<li>Visa or e-visa approval printed, along with the invitation letter we provide for trekking and mountaineering visas.</li>
<li>Travel insurance documents that explicitly cover your maximum altitude and, for treks and expeditions, helicopter evacuation.</li>
<li>Passport photographs — useful for permits and registration.</li>
<li>Cash. Card payment is limited outside the main cities, and there are no ATMs beyond the larger towns. Bring enough rupees for tips, local purchases and emergencies.</li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>Clothing: the layer system</strong></h2>
<p>Three layers handle everything in the Karakoram: a base layer that moves sweat away, a warm mid layer, and a shell that stops wind and rain. Add or remove as the day changes, and it will change a great deal between a glacier morning and a valley afternoon.</p>
<h3><strong>For cultural tours and jeep journeys</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Comfortable trousers and long-sleeved shirts; loose and modest clothing is appreciated in villages.</li>
<li>A warm fleece or light down jacket — valley nights are cold even in summer.</li>
<li>A light waterproof jacket.</li>
<li>Walking shoes or trainers with grip.</li>
<li>Sun hat, sunglasses, high-factor sunscreen and lip balm with sun protection.</li>
<li>A scarf or shawl, which women travellers often find useful for visits to mosques and villages.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>For trekking</strong></h3>
<p>Everything above, plus:</p>
<ul>
<li>Broken-in trekking boots with ankle support. Never bring new boots.</li>
<li>Two or three pairs of trekking socks and liner socks.</li>
<li>Insulated jacket for camp, warm hat and gloves.</li>
<li>Waterproof jacket and over-trousers.</li>
<li>Four-season sleeping bag rated well below the coldest night you expect, plus a sleeping mat.</li>
<li>Trekking poles — they make a real difference on glacier moraine.</li>
<li>Headtorch with spare batteries.</li>
<li>Daypack of 25 to 35 litres, and a duffle bag for the porter load.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>For expeditions</strong></h3>
<p>Add high-altitude boots, a down suit, a harness, crampons, an ice axe, glacier glasses and the personal climbing kit your route requires. Our full <a href="/equipment-checklist/">trekking and mountaineering equipment checklist</a> covers this in detail by altitude.</p>

<h2><strong>Health and hygiene</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li>Personal medication in original packaging, with enough for the whole trip and a few spare days.</li>
<li>Basic kit: painkillers, blister plasters, antiseptic, rehydration salts, anti-diarrhoeal, antihistamine.</li>
<li>Anything your doctor recommends for altitude — discuss this before you travel, not on the mountain.</li>
<li>Hand sanitiser and wet wipes; washing facilities are limited on trek.</li>
<li>Water purification tablets or a filter bottle. Our camps boil water, but a backup is sensible on the road.</li>
<li>High-factor sunscreen. At altitude the sun is far stronger than the temperature suggests.</li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>Electronics and power</strong></h2>
<p>Pakistan uses 220V with round-pin plugs. Electricity in the valleys can be intermittent, and on trek there is none beyond what you carry or what the base camp generator provides.</p>
<ul>
<li>A power bank, or two for longer treks. Cold drains batteries quickly — keep them in your sleeping bag at night.</li>
<li>Solar charger if you are camping for more than a week.</li>
<li>Spare memory cards; there is nowhere to back up once you leave Skardu.</li>
<li>A local SIM is worth buying in Islamabad. Coverage reaches most valleys but disappears entirely on the glaciers.</li>
<li>Drones need permission in Pakistan. Ask us well before you travel rather than arriving with one.</li>
</ul>

<h2><strong>What you can leave at home</strong></h2>
<p>Heavy towels, large toiletry bottles, several changes of smart clothing, and anything you are &quot;probably&quot; going to use. On trek every extra kilo is carried by someone, and porter loads are limited by agreement.</p>
<p>Some items can be hired or bought in Skardu rather than flown in: sleeping bags, down jackets, mats and trekking poles are generally available, and quality varies, so tell us in advance what you would rather hire and we will confirm what we can arrange.</p>

<h2><strong>A note on dress</strong></h2>
<p>Gilgit-Baltistan is relaxed by Pakistani standards and used to visitors, but it is still a conservative region. Loose clothing that covers shoulders and knees is appropriate for both men and women in villages and towns. A scarf is useful for women at religious sites. None of this is enforced on travellers, but it is noticed and appreciated.</p>

<h2><strong>Before you close the bag</strong></h2>
<p>Weigh it. Domestic flights to Skardu have strict baggage limits, and the difference between a comfortable trek and a miserable one is often just a few kilos. If you are unsure about any item, send us your itinerary and we will tell you whether you will use it. Our <a href="/faqs/">travel FAQs</a> answer the questions that come up most often before departure.</p>

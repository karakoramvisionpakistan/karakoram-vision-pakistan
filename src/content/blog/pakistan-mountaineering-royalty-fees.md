<h2><strong>The short answer</strong></h2>
<p>Every expedition to a major peak in Gilgit-Baltistan pays a royalty, the fee for the right to climb the mountain. It depends on the height of the peak:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>K2 (8,611 m):</strong> USD 3,500</li>
<li><strong>Other 8,000-metre peaks</strong> (Nanga Parbat, Gasherbrum I, Broad Peak, Gasherbrum II): USD 2,500</li>
<li><strong>Peaks of 7,000 metres and above:</strong> USD 2,000</li>
<li><strong>Trekking peaks from 6,500 to 7,000 metres:</strong> a small fee</li>
<li><strong>Peaks below 6,500 metres:</strong> climbed on a trekking permit</li>
</ul>
<p>The rates are set by the Gilgit-Baltistan government and can change between seasons. We confirm the current figure in writing when we prepare your permit application.</p>
<h2><strong>What the royalty is, and what it is not</strong></h2>
<p>The royalty pays for the permit to climb one named peak by one route. It is not the cost of the expedition. A team on an 8,000-metre peak also pays for a liaison officer who travels with the expedition, an environment fee, porters to base camp, the base camp itself and, if it uses them, oxygen and high-altitude porters. Our <a href="/expeditions/k2-expedition/">K2 expedition</a> page lists what we provide on the mountain.</p>
<p>The royalty is paid in Pakistan after the mountaineering visa is granted. The permit application goes in through a licensed operator, several months before the season. Our <a href="/blog/pakistan-trekking-permits-visa/">permit and visa guide</a> sets out the steps and the timing.</p>
<h2><strong>K2: USD 3,500</strong></h2>
<p>K2 is the only peak in Pakistan with a royalty band of its own. The expedition approaches along the Baltoro Glacier to base camp at about 5,100 metres on the Godwin-Austen Glacier, and most teams climb the Abruzzi Spur.</p>
<h2><strong>The other 8,000-metre peaks: USD 2,500</strong></h2>
<p>Pakistan has four more peaks above 8,000 metres, and each carries the same royalty:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="/expeditions/nanga-parbat-expedition/">Nanga Parbat</a> (8,126 m)</li>
<li><a href="/expeditions/gasherbrum-i-expedition/">Gasherbrum I</a> (8,080 m)</li>
<li><a href="/expeditions/broad-peak-expedition/">Broad Peak</a> (8,051 m)</li>
<li><a href="/expeditions/gasherbrum-ii-expedition/">Gasherbrum II</a> (8,035 m)</li>
</ul>
<p>A permit covers one peak. Teams that climb Broad Peak and then K2 in the same season pay two royalties, but they share the approach, the base camp and much of the acclimatisation, so the second peak costs far less than a second expedition. See all our <a href="/expeditions/8000m-peaks/">8000m peak expeditions</a>.</p>
<h2><strong>7,000-metre peaks: USD 2,000</strong></h2>
<p>Peaks from 7,000 metres up to 8,000 metres pay a royalty of USD 2,000 and are climbed on a mountaineering permit, in the same way as the higher peaks. Our <a href="/expeditions/spantik-peak-expedition/">Spantik expedition</a> (7,027 m) is in this band.</p>
<h2><strong>6,500 to 7,000 metres: trekking peaks</strong></h2>
<p>Peaks from 6,500 to 7,000 metres are classed as trekking peaks. They carry a small fee rather than a full royalty. We confirm the current amount when we apply.</p>
<h2><strong>Below 6,500 metres: a trekking permit</strong></h2>
<p>Peaks below 6,500 metres are climbed on a trekking permit, which your operator applies for, and in restricted zones you climb with a licensed guide. This is how most people start climbing in Pakistan: <a href="/expeditions/laila-peak-expedition/">Laila Peak</a> (6,096 m) above the Hushe valley and <a href="/trekking/khosar-gang-peak/">Khosar Gang</a> in Shigar are both climbed this way.</p>

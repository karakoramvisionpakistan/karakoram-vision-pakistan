<h2><strong>Why insurance matters more here</strong></h2>
<p>Most treks in northern Pakistan, including the <a href="/trekking/k2-base-camp-trek/">K2 base camp trek</a> and the <a href="/trekking/gondogoro-la-circuit-trek/">Gondogoro La trek</a>, run for days beyond the last road. If someone is badly hurt or seriously ill with altitude, the fastest way out is a helicopter, and that is paid for in advance, not after the event. Good travel insurance is what makes that call possible.</p>
<p>For our treks and expeditions, insurance that covers your maximum altitude and helicopter search and rescue is compulsory. We ask for your policy details before the trek starts.</p>
<h2><strong>What your policy must cover</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Trekking at your highest altitude.</strong> Many standard policies exclude trekking above a set height. K2 base camp is about 5,100 metres and the Gondogoro La about 5,585 metres, so check the altitude clause against the highest point of your route.</li>
<li><strong>Helicopter search and rescue.</strong> Make sure it is named in the policy, not just "emergency evacuation".</li>
<li><strong>Emergency medical treatment and repatriation</strong> to your home country.</li>
<li><strong>Trip cancellation and curtailment</strong>, so flights and deposits are protected if you have to cancel.</li>
<li><strong>Baggage and personal equipment</strong>, if you are bringing expensive gear.</li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>How helicopter rescue works in Pakistan</strong></h2>
<p>There are no private rescue helicopters on standby in the Karakoram. Rescues are coordinated by Askari Aviation using Pakistan Army Aviation helicopters. Before a rescue can fly, the climber or trekker must be registered and the cost guaranteed, by a cash security deposit, a bank guarantee from the local tour operator, or a written guarantee from your embassy. The deposit is refunded, less a service charge, if it is not used.</p>
<p>Rescue is not guaranteed: the helicopters fly only when they are not needed for other duties, and weather often decides. That is why a good itinerary has acclimatization days and a guide who turns back early, and why insurance is a safety net, not a plan. Expedition teams arrange this as part of the permit; for treks we explain how it works for your group before departure.</p>
<h2><strong>Before you buy</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li>Read the altitude clause and the list of excluded activities.</li>
<li>Check whether your government's travel advice for the region affects your cover.</li>
<li>Save the insurer's 24-hour emergency number and your policy number on your phone and on paper.</li>
<li>Send us the policy details; our guide carries them on the trek.</li>
<li>If you are climbing a peak, check that mountaineering is covered, not only trekking.</li>
</ul>

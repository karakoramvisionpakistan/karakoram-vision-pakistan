<h2><strong>The vehicle we use most in the mountains</strong></h2><p>If a journey involves leaving the tarmac, it involves a Prado. It takes the Karakoram Highway at highway speed and then keeps going where the surface stops being a road at all &mdash; the Deosai tracks, Shigar and Basho, the Hushe road, the Astore side &mdash; carrying four or five people and their luggage in reasonable comfort.</p><p>That combination is rarer than it sounds. A saloon car manages the highway and nothing beyond it; a jeep manages the tracks and punishes you on the highway. The Prado does both, which is why most of our private itineraries are built around one.</p><h2><strong>Where the road stops being a road</strong></h2><p>It is worth being specific about what these valleys actually involve, because &ldquo;4x4 required&rdquo; covers a wide range of things.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Loose surfaces cut into cliffs</strong> &mdash; single track in places, with a long drop and no barrier. The Braldu road to Askole is the extreme example.</p></li><li><p><strong>Unbridged stream crossings</strong> &mdash; passable in the morning and running much higher by afternoon as the glaciers melt. Timing matters more than ground clearance.</p></li><li><p><strong>Snow and mud on the high plateaus</strong> &mdash; Deosai in early and late season, where the question is not whether the vehicle can do it but whether the track is open at all.</p></li></ul><h2><strong>Why we do not offer self-drive</strong></h2><p>We say this plainly because we are asked regularly. We do not hire these vehicles out without a driver in these valleys, and no visiting driver should attempt the high roads alone on a first trip.</p><p>It is not a question of skill at the wheel. It is local knowledge: which crossing is safe at which hour, which section slides after rain, where the road has moved since last season, and who to call when something goes wrong a hundred kilometres from the nearest workshop. Our drivers are from these valleys and have driven them for years.</p><h2><strong>How it is hired</strong></h2><p>By the day, for a single transfer such as Skardu airport, or for a full itinerary running several weeks. Fuel, the driver allowance and tolls are set out in the written quote, so nothing is settled at the roadside.</p><p>For larger parties we mix vehicles &mdash; a van or a Coaster on the main highway and jeeps for the rough sections &mdash; so that nobody is paying for a 4x4 on days that do not need one. What the hire includes, including driver hours, fuel, tolls, road permissions and any night away from base, is stated in the written quotation.</p><h2><strong>Related</strong></h2><ul><li><p><a href="/vehicles/toyota-hiace-van/">Toyota Hiace van</a> &mdash; when the party is larger and the surface stays sealed</p></li><li><p><a href="/jeep-safaris/deosai-jeep-safari/">Deosai jeep safari</a> &mdash; a trip built on exactly this ground</p></li><li><p><a href="/destinations/skardu/">Skardu travel guide</a> &mdash; where most of these journeys begin</p></li></ul>

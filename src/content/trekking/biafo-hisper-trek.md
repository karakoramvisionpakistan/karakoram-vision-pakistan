<h2><strong>About this journey</strong></h2><p>Very few treks anywhere cross an entire mountain range on ice. The Biafo Hispar traverse links Baltistan with Hunza along 116 kilometres of connected glacier &mdash; the longest glacial system outside the polar regions &mdash; crossing the Hispar La at 5,151 metres and passing through Snow Lake, the great white basin that early Karakoram explorers could not account for.</p><p>The route starts at Askole, the roadhead for the Baltoro, and then turns north instead of east. Nine days go up the Biafo to the pass, with a rest day at the Baintha oasis; one long day crosses Snow Lake and reaches the Hispar La; and five days descend the Hispar glacier to the first village on the Nagar side. From there it is jeep and highway to Karimabad, Hunza and home.</p><p>There is no technical climbing. There is roped glacier travel for about a week, a pass to cross on snow, and no way out once the team is committed to the upper Biafo except forward or back the way it came. That is the character of the trek, and it is why people do it.</p><h2><strong>Snow Lake</strong></h2><p>Snow Lake &mdash; Lukpe Lawo in Balti &mdash; is the reason this traverse has a place in mountaineering history rather than just in brochures.</p><p>It is a basin of snow roughly sixteen kilometres across at around 4,900 metres, almost level, enclosed by peaks on every side, where the heads of the Biafo and Hispar glaciers meet and part. Martin Conway reached it in 1892, named it, and came home with the idea that it might be the remnant of an inland ice sheet. Eric Shipton and Bill Tilman came to survey it properly in 1937 and could not entirely explain it either.</p><p>Walking across it is a strange experience by any standard. There is no feature to measure distance against, no sound at all once the group stops, and the peaks around the rim stay the same size for hours. It is also a crevasse field under snow, which is why the team is roped and why the guide&rsquo;s line is followed exactly.</p><h2><strong>Who this trek suits</strong></h2><p>Experienced trekkers who are comfortable with long days on rough ground, with being roped on a glacier, and with three weeks away from roads, shops and mobile signal. No climbing skill is needed and none is taught; what is needed is hill fitness built over months rather than weeks, and a steady temperament.</p><p>The commitment is the part to think about honestly. From the day the team moves onto the upper Biafo, the nearest road is days away in either direction, and the only way to leave is on your own feet. Nothing about that is dangerous in itself. It does mean a blister ignored at Namla becomes a serious problem at Morphogoro.</p><p>Our own classification for this route is <strong>strenuous</strong>. Twenty-two days, a 5,151-metre pass, a week of roped travel and twelve nights under canvas are not a moderate undertaking, whatever the absence of technical climbing might suggest.</p><h2><strong>When to go</strong></h2><p><strong>1 July to the end of August.</strong> The window is narrow and the reason is the snow.</p><p>Snow Lake and the upper Biafo are crossed on snow bridges over crevasses. Too early in the season and there is too much unconsolidated snow and the bridges are unreliable; too late and they have melted thin. July and August are when the surface is at its most dependable and the weather at its most settled. Outside those two months this traverse is not a trekking proposition.</p><p>Daytime walking in the lower Biafo is warm and the glare off the ice is severe. Nights above 4,000 metres are below freezing, and the camp on the pass is colder than anything else on the route.</p><h2><strong>What we handle</strong></h2><p>Permits and the park fee, trekker registration in Skardu, transport from Islamabad to Askole and from Hispar village back to Islamabad, porters for both sides of the traverse, all camp equipment, a cook and kitchen crew, and guides who have crossed the Hispar La before &mdash; which on this route matters more than on most, because the line across Snow Lake changes from season to season.</p><p>Porters are engaged separately for the two halves, since the Askole men do not cross to Nagar and the Hispar men do not come east. The changeover is arranged in advance, and it is one of the things that makes this traverse harder to organise than an out-and-back trek.</p><p>Every porter and staff member is insured through Alpha Insurance before the trek leaves Skardu, arranged and paid for by us.</p><h2><strong>Facts and figures</strong></h2><ul><li><p><strong>Duration</strong> &mdash; 22 days, Islamabad to Islamabad</p></li><li><p><strong>Nights</strong> &mdash; 10 in hotels, 12 under canvas</p></li><li><p><strong>Highest point</strong> &mdash; Hispar La, 5,151 m</p></li><li><p><strong>Glacier</strong> &mdash; 116 km of connected ice, Biafo and Hispar combined</p></li><li><p><strong>Grade</strong> &mdash; strenuous; no technical climbing, roped glacier travel throughout the upper sections</p></li><li><p><strong>Season</strong> &mdash; 1 July to the end of August</p></li><li><p><strong>Zone</strong> &mdash; open; no restricted-area permit required</p></li></ul><h2><strong>Wildlife</strong></h2><p>The Biafo is one of the better places in the Karakoram to see large animals, and Baintha in particular. <strong>Himalayan brown bear</strong> live in this section of the glacier&rsquo;s ablation valleys and their tracks are found near the camp regularly; actual sightings are a matter of luck and patience. Ibex are on the crags above the moraine, and snow leopard are present in these valleys although very few trekkers ever see one.</p><p>Flowers in the ablation valleys are at their best in July, and the Baintha bay carries willow, juniper scrub and alpine herbs in a place that is otherwise forty kilometres of rock and ice. Take nothing and leave nothing: this is inside the Central Karakoram National Park and the ground recovers slowly.</p><h2><strong>Equipment</strong></h2><p>A full list arrives with your booking confirmation. Four things matter more than the rest of it put together.</p><ul><li><p><strong>Boots</strong> &mdash; stiff-soled, supportive, crampon-compatible, and worn in over months rather than weeks. On a route with no road access for a fortnight, a boot that does not fit is what ends a trek.</p></li><li><p><strong>Sleeping bag and mat</strong> &mdash; the camp on the Hispar La is the coldest night of the trip and everything above Baintha is below freezing after dark. Rent a bag here if you would rather not fly with one.</p></li><li><p><strong>Glacier glasses</strong> &mdash; side shields, not ordinary sunglasses. A week of reflected light off Snow Lake is precisely how snow blindness happens.</p></li><li><p><strong>Sun cream, generously and often</strong> &mdash; under the nose, under the chin, inside the ears. At this altitude the glare arrives from below as well as above.</p></li></ul><p>Harness, rope and the glacier hardware are issued and fitted in Skardu, and your guide takes the group through the technique during the rest day at Baintha. Bring your own harness if there is one you already trust. Finally, bring something to keep a phone or camera alive &mdash; a battery pack, and a folding panel if you own one. Nothing between Askole and Karimabad runs on mains electricity.</p><h2><strong>Insurance</strong></h2><p><strong>Cover is mandatory on this traverse and arranging it falls to you, not to us.</strong> The policy has to reach helicopter rescue at these altitudes, hospital treatment, and repatriation. The cover bundled with an ordinary holiday reaches none of the three, and neither do most annual multi-trip policies without a specific mountaineering extension.</p><p>Pass us the policy number and whichever contact the insurer staffs around the clock, well ahead of departure. If something happens on the Hispar, that number is dialled before anything else, and no aircraft lifts off until the insurer has confirmed cover. Finding out at Kani Basa that a policy excludes rescue above 4,500 metres is the worst possible moment to learn it.</p><h2><strong>Getting there and getting back</strong></h2><p>This traverse is unusual in finishing five hundred road kilometres from where it began. The group travels out through Baltistan to Skardu and Askole, and returns from Hispar village and Karimabad down the Karakoram Highway through Hunza. It is a loop rather than an out-and-back, and it shows you two parts of Gilgit-Baltistan that have little in common beyond the mountains between them.</p><p>Both road sections are long, spectacular and at the mercy of conditions in their own way &mdash; the Braldu jeep track closes for rockfall, and the Babusar pass is a summer-only road. The programme is laid out with that built in. One request, though: give yourself a clear day in Islamabad at the end rather than connecting straight onto a long-haul flight the same night.</p>
